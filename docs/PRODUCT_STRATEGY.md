# Oja — Product Strategy

**Version:** 0.1 (Draft) · **Last updated:** 2026-07-09

---

## 1. Strategic Thesis

The African food category in North America is supply-constrained not because food is scarce, but because **demand is illegible**. Whoever makes demand legible controls the category: they buy better, ship leaner, waste less, and price fairer than anyone guessing.

So Oja's strategy is deliberately ordered:

1. **Own the demand signal** (pantry profiles, standing orders, sell-through) —
2. **so the JIT network outperforms** (less spoilage, fewer stockouts, leaner working capital) —
3. **so unit economics beat incumbents** (pass some savings to customers, keep some as margin) —
4. **so more demand consolidates onto Oja** — which sharpens the signal further. A compounding data flywheel.

Logistics is the visible product; the demand graph is the company.

## 2. The Moat (in order of durability)

| Moat | Why it compounds |
|---|---|
| Demand graph (SKU × household × region × season) | Every cycle-confirm, skip, and substitution labels the dataset; no incumbent has per-household African-staple consumption data at any scale |
| Committed demand % | Subscriptions and standing orders make forecast partially *contractual*, not statistical — structurally impossible for a spot-sales wholesaler to copy |
| Supplier allocation | Forward visibility earns priority allocation and quality specs from the best importers/processors |
| Community trust & brand | Church/association distribution is relationship-gated; slow to build, slow to lose |
| Network density | Route density per zone drops delivery cost per order; winner-take-most per metro |

## 3. Product Tiers

### B2C
- **Oja Market** (free) — one-off ordering at retail prices.
- **Oja Pantry** (subscription) — recurring basket with cycle-confirm flow, member pricing (~5–8% below Market), freshness guarantee, priority delivery windows. *The flagship — this is where demand signal comes from.*
- **Oja Together** (group buying) — community/church group orders at near-wholesale tiers; conversion funnel into Pantry.

### B2B
- **Oja Wholesale** (free tier) — wholesale catalog, weekly delivery, MOV applies.
- **Oja Wholesale+** (paid tier) — standing-order templates, sell-through analytics dashboard, net-15/30 terms, dedicated rep, stockout-protection SLA on contracted top-SKUs.

### Supply side
- **Oja Supplier Portal** (Phase 2+) — demand forecasts shared per SKU, forward commitments, faster payment rails. Fee-free initially; monetize later via financing/priority programs.

## 4. Roadmap Arc

**Phase 1 — Prove the loop (0–3 mo, one metro):** curated catalog, pantry profiles, heuristic forecast v0, one cross-dock, batch delivery. Success = 60% of volume from committed demand, spoilage < 8%.

**Phase 2 — Make the engine real (4–9 mo):** ML forecast v1 with events/seasonality, POS sell-through integrations, second metro, group buying productized, supplier portal alpha. Success = WAPE < 25%, metro-2 launch cost < 50% of metro-1's.

**Phase 3 — Move up the chain (10–18 mo):** container-consolidation planning from aggregate forecast, dynamic markdown/expiry pricing, cold-chain assortment, Toronto. Success = import-level POs driven by Oja forecast; category-leading fresh assortment in 2 metros.

**Phase 4 — Category OS (18+ mo):** white-label replenishment for independent stores (stores become nodes: pickup points + micro-fulfillment), supplier financing against committed demand, private-label staples where quality/margin justify.

## 5. Product Principles

1. **Signal over sale.** Any feature that increases legible, committed demand outranks a feature that only increases one-off GMV.
2. **Confirm, don't surprise.** Recurring commerce in this category must be reviewable (cycle-confirm), or trust dies with the first unwanted box.
3. **Stores are partners.** Every B2C capability should have a version that makes a store owner stronger, not obsolete.
4. **Perishability is a first-class dimension.** Catalog, forecast, pricing, and picking logic all branch on perishability class — never bolt it on.
5. **WhatsApp-real.** Meet users in the channels and languages they actually use; the web app is the system of record, not the only interface.
6. **Instrument everything from day one.** The demand engine's ceiling is set by what we log in month one.

## 6. Competitive Landscape

- **Ethnic online resellers (Weee!-style, African niche shops):** strong on assortment breadth, weak on freshness/price/reliability; none own B2B or demand data. Oja wins on reliability + price via JIT.
- **Traditional importers/wholesalers:** own supply relationships, zero digital demand capture; more likely acquisition targets/partners than defensible competitors.
- **Mainstream grocery delivery (Instacart et al.):** thin African assortment via partner stores; no supply-chain ownership. Coexists.
- **Real risk:** a well-capitalized ethnic-grocery platform adding an African vertical. Defense: community distribution + committed-demand contracts + supplier allocation, fast.

## 7. Strategic Bets & Kill Criteria

| Bet | Kill/pivot signal |
|---|---|
| Households will commit to pantry subscriptions | Cycle-confirm rate < 60% after 3 iterations of the flow |
| Committed demand makes JIT viable at micro-warehouse scale | Spoilage + stockout costs exceed carrying-cost savings after 6 months |
| Stores will share sell-through for better replenishment | < 30% of Wholesale+ accounts logging data by month 9 → lean harder on our own shipment data instead |
| Community-institution channel beats paid acquisition | Blended community CAC exceeds paid CAC for 2 consecutive quarters |
